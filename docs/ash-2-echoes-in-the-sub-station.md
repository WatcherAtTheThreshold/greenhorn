**Echoes in the Sub-Station**

Ash finished packing his bag and slung it over his shoulder. The fire was low but still burning, Tilly wasn't there. He slid his backpack off of his shoulder and set it back on the ground. Gathering sticks he threw them on top of the coals, the flames wrapping around them.

 Ash called Tilly’s name; a large crow launched from a nearby tree, its wings whooshing as it flew away. Rising above the tree tops it disappeared from view. 

"Ash, I'm almost there," her voice came from the woods. A few seconds later Tilly pushed through the underbrush holding a fish in each hand, "I caught breakfast\!" 

Ash smiled. "Perfect timing. How did you catch those?"

"I woke up early, I've been fishing since before sunrise." Tilly’s furry Kithren face shining with pride. She fashioned roasting skewers and set both fish to cook over the flames.

They had been traveling for weeks. Tilly had a slight limp, Ash thought she might be walking on blisters. And they were nearly out of supplies. We'll stop in the next town, he thought.

They both sat on their packs. Tilly turned the fish over and held her hands out to the fire to warm them.

Ash looked to Tilly and said "We'll stop in the next town we come across, replenish supplies, maybe wash our clothes."

"That would be great," said Tilly, turning the fish again, "These are looking good, almost done," she rubbed her belly.

Tilly handed one of the skewers to Ash; it smelled buttery and sweet.

They finished eating and put their packs and bedrolls on. Extinguishing the fire they walked towards the main road. 

Birds sang and  squirrels ran about, filling the air with chatter. The day was starting out warm, haze softened the view. Stands of trees were speckled between hills and fields, fading off to gentle blue rolling mountains in  the distance. 

Tilly motioned to Ash, "What do you think these tracks are?" 

Ash looked, "Hmm... They're small, cloven-hooved, I don't know what made them though, smaller than a deer."

---

Walking east meant that the sun was in their eyes every morning, so they didn't see the structure ahead until they were upon it. It was a sprawling one story building, made of concrete. Tall, golden, grasses with bristle heads grew from the gutters. The door was rusted and broken; there was a hole where the doorknob should have been. Little Kithren footprints led into and out of the building.

Ash pushed the door open, the dust coated hinges creaked.

Tilly took a step backward. "We shouldn't go in, it looks dangerous." 

Ash peaked his head into the door, "let's just look, the door's open." He stepped inside, it smelled of metal and dust. He looked around, eyes adjusting to the dark. It was just one large room. Machines and counter-tops lined the walls. Slowly forms came into view; there were many tables with many machines. Something on the floor at the center of the room caught his eye, an orb.

Tilly said, "I'll stay out here," not trusting the sharp metallic smells of the place. 

"There's something in the center of the room, I'm gonna check it out." Ash looked at Tilly. 

She nodded her head in silent agreement. "Be careful," she said.

Ash walked on the undisturbed dust towards the object. There was a perfect sphere half submerged in the floor. Ash reached out and touched it, the sphere started emitting a soft glow. Machines around the room came to life. Ash had a panicked look on his face. 

A female voice spoke over a loudspeaker, "Foundry-node 47... standby... rebooting..." Pulsing light cascaded down the sides of the glowing sphere.

Panicked, Ash said, "don't come in here\!"

"I won't," said Tilly, breathing heavily. "You should come out of there." She made a 'come here' motion with her hands. Ash started to carefully walk back to the door. 

On the other side of the room something slid open. Ash turned to see what it was and bumped into a table. Two heavy feet stepped onto the floor. Ash froze, heart pounding.

A calm modulated voice crackled from  across the room, "hello, is anyone there?" The lights flickered on, a robot was looking at Ash. "Your identification please," said the bot in a calm, monotone voice. 

Ash tried to run but slipped in the thick dust. He fell onto the ground landing hard on his hip. A thick cloud of dust engulfed him; he choked and coughed, dust stung his eyes blinding him. The robot walked closer.

Tilly ran in, grabbing Ash by the hand and pulled him towards the door. He crawled forward trying to stand up, blind, slipping on the smooth dust. 

The Robot pressed forward. "Do you have identification?"

Tilly looked at it with startled eyes and pulled Ash frantically. He regained his footing and they stumbled out of the door into the daylight. They were prepared to run as the robot closed the distance. He didn't walk out of the door; he started walking around checking on machines. Other smaller robots emerged into the room making whooshing noises; they were leaving clean paths as they went. Tilly and Ash watched, marveling at the procession.

Ash, covered in dust, started wiping and beating at his pants and shirt, grey clouds billowed and blew away in the wind. He removed his cloak and shook another cloud of dust from it.

The robot stepped out of the door. "Are either of you technicians?" it asked.

"No," they said in unison. 

"We are travelers," said Ash. "Just traveling by," he looked at Tilly with a sly smile, raising his eyebrows and shoulders. 

The robot removed the door from its hinges and carried it around the side of the building. He came back around the corner without the door and went back inside. 

Ash looked at Tilly, raising his eyebrows and shoulders again, this time with a questioning frown. Tilly smiled, they watched the robot team cleaning the large dusty station. One of the small cleaning bots rolled past the doorway, its twirling bristles spinning beneath. 

Ash said, "I guess we should keep going," motioning towards the road.

"What's that?" asked Tilly, noticing something westward down the road. "Was someone walking behind us?" A lone figure approached. "We haven't been here long, they must have been close behind."

"Probably better if we wait here to see who they are, hopefully they just keep walking," said Ash. "Maybe it’s a technician? Win win," he chuckled.

---

They were both nervous as the character drew near. At first it appeared to be a cloaked human but as it got closer they saw it was a robot. 

"I think that's the robot I saw on the road before I got to your village. He was motionless, I thought he was broken." Ash didn't know what to make of it 'following' them. "Hello\!" he said loud enough for the approaching robot to hear. Ash felt Tilly move closer to him, she bumped into his leg.

"Hello?" the robot called back, voice crackling and modulating in a wavering cadence.

Ash and Tilly looked at one another, both looked happily surprised by how friendly he sounded. 

The robot said, "I’m here to access this foundry. I'm a technician." The robot walked right past them into the building. They watched the technician introduce himself to the foundry bot, who proceeded to run some sort of diagnostics on him.  

Tilly turned to Ash and asked, "did that really just happen?" She looked at the robots in the foundry, then back at him. 

 "Coincidence, I guess," said Ash. They both laughed. 

They watched as the technician stepped into a large cabinet, the cabinet doors slid shut, enclosing him. The foundry robot was performing maintenance on pieces of machinery. The cleaning bots were cleaning; they had flexible tubes that looked like limbs, that were sucking the dust off of tables and countertops.

The doors to the technicians cabinet slid open and he walked out. He walked across the room right towards them. 

---

"I'm Ash," said Ash, it was all he could think of doing.

"I'm Tilly," she piped in.

"I'm Timothy," said  the technician. He turned and started walking away across the large field.

"Wait a sec," said Ash. "Where are you going?"

Timothy stopped and turned, "My power-core is nearly depleted, the foundry cannot replicate a new one. There is a power sub-station a few miles to the south of here, there may be a power-core there." He turned and started walking again. 

Ash looked at Tilly, eyebrows raised, silently asking if they should go; Tilly shook her head yes. 

"Can we come along, can we help?" asked Ash.

"Sure, my systems are faltering, I could shut down again." Timothy reasoned.

---

The three of them walked across the field and into the woods, sticks and leaves crunched under foot. Timothy was moving a bit slow and his voice had a slight treble.

Timothy turned to them and asked "Why did you two decide to come with me?" 

Ash thought for a moment, "We're adventurers, it sounded like an adventure.”.

"And we're headed east to train under great knights," added Tilly.

Timothy tilted his head and said "The knights Artorius and Siff? I live in the east, I know them, they are an imposing duo." 

Ash and Tilly exchanged glances. Ash asked, "Do you know the knight Orion?"

"No, he lives in the east as well but I've never met him. I know Siff and Artorius because I'm friends with their daughter Magdaline."

After walking for hours, through wooded areas, through fields, across streams, they came upon a rise where they could see a building in the distance with a tall antenna rising from its roof. 

Timothy pointed to it across the valley and said, "there it is, the sub-station."

They navigated down the wooded hillside, leaf litter slippery under foot. The sun was tipping to the west lengthening the shadows. 

Ash got Tilly's attention and pointed out a track in the mud. It looked like the same cloven track that she had pointed out on the road.

They could see activity at the sub station, It looked like there were some Kithren moving about.

"Hello," Timothy called to the station in his wavering cadence, his voice sounded like it was getting worse.

A voice called from the sub-station across the valley. "You're gonna want to get over here before the demon pigs come."  

The three picked up their pace but Timothy was having trouble keeping up. His metal foot slipped and wedged between two logs. They stopped while he pulled and tried to wiggle it free. Ash lifted one of the logs to help free his foot.

They walked up to the sub-station and a small group of Kithren stood outside.

---

"You will have to come inside with us, I am Gulnare, what are the three of you doing way out here?" Gulnare and his companions looked at the three of them with suspicion. 

Timothy stepped forward and said, "I am Timothy and these are my friends, Ash and Tilly,"

Gulnare and his group were a bit larger than Tilly's people, waist tall to Ash. This group had large canines, giving them a menacing look, they were furry faced and had a dark mask of fur around their eyes.

 Timothy asked Gulnare, "What are the demon pigs?"

"They will be here soon, as soon as the sun is below the mountains, before it gets dark," said one of Gulnare's companions. "These pigs have been terrorizing our village for more than a year; we left to get supplies and maybe find a solution. They trapped us here two days ago. We barely made it here and we don't know if we'd make it back."

Ash saw more of the tracks on the ground that were like the ones he and Tilly had seen on the road. Demon pigs, he thought to himself.

As the sun sank behind the mountains they started to hear snorting, grunting and  barking noises approaching.

Gulnare motioned and his companions followed him with Tilly, Ash and Timothy behind. "Let's go inside, we'll be able to see them through the windows."  Gulnare's group shut and barred the door once everyone was inside. They all climbed a metal staircase to get a view outside and watched as furry pigs started to appear from behind trees. They were heading straight towards them. 

Timothy counted more than fifty of these ‘demon pigs’ appearing out of the forest. The peccaries, the little pigs, ran up to the building, sniffing and snorting at the door, some ramming at it with their stout heads. 

"I don't know," said Timothy, "I'm picking up strange power sources, I think something is making them aggressive. We should explore, find a power-core and maybe we'll find the energy source that's driving them crazy. I am picking up a high pitched frequency."

---

"This building has underground levels we haven't explored," said Gulnare to Timothy. "It's really dark down there."

Ash said, "we have a lantern," Tilly took her backpack off and dug out the lantern she carried. She removed the flint and tinder and prepared to light it.

Timothy stopped her saying "I can ignite that,"  holding a single finger out. Tilly opened the glass door and held the lanterns exposed wick towards him. He lit the wick with his finger. A thin stream of black smoke rose from the flame. The smell of soot filled the air. 

Everyone in Gulnare's group looked at one another, eyes wide, heads nodding yes.

Timothy motioned to the stairwell. "We should go." 

Tilly handed the lantern to Timothy, he opened the stairwell door and started walking down the stairs. The metal creaked below his feet. Everyone followed: Timothy, then Gulnare and his group, then Tilly, with Ash taking up the rear.

At the bottom  of the stairs was a door. Timothy tried to turn the knob, it was locked. He put a finger against the lock, making buzzing and whizzing noises, then the lock clicked. Timothy turned the knob, it opened.

---

They walked through the door, the room on the other side looked exactly like the foundry. Timothy walked to the center of the room and touched the orb there. 

A female voice, the same as the one at the foundry said "sub-station-node 50... standby... rebooting..." The glowing sphere pulsed, light cascading down its sides. The overhead lights flickered on. Doors slid open on a cabinet that sat against one of the walls. 

Timothy stepped forward looking into the empty cabinet, "the steward is not here\!"

Tilly asked Timothy, "Do you need the steward to fix your power-core?" 

"I do not need the steward to replace my  power-core, this station has the needed resources  to replicate one." Timothy replied. "I wonder where he went."

Timothy stepped into the cabinet, the doors slid closed. Three cleaning bots came out of small cubicles and got to work, buzzing and whirring. Gulnare's group looked startled. 

Gulnare with a snarl said, "Anora, my daughter, stay by me."

"Don't worry," said Tilly, "They're just cleaners, we've seen them before." She approached one of the bots to show it wasn't dangerous. Ash nodded his head in agreement.

The cleaning bots all stopped suddenly. Tilly noticed and nudged Ash, pointing to one of them.

The door to the hallway slid shut and the lights dimmed to an eerie golden amber. Tilly ran to the door that led to the hallway, it was locked.

Ash called to the closed cabinet doors, "Timothy\! The power is out and the door to the ladder is locked, are you okay? Did you get fixed in there?" 

"My power-core has not been replaced; the maintenance chamber seems to have lost power." Timothy's voice was muffled talking through the chamber door. "I think the steward must have gone down the maintenance shaft. If I'm correct he may have gone to repair something and something went wrong. It could be dangerous but I think one of you may need to go down to see if you can help."

Ash called in to Timothy, "I'll go down," he said, with a resolute look on his face. "Where's the hatch?" 

---

Gulnares' companions looked at one another, "We will go to," he growled.

Timothy said, "The hatch is near this chamber module that I'm trapped in, you should see a grate on the floor." 

The Kithren found the hatch grate. With one on each corner they lifted it off of the floor. Ash took the lantern and climbed down the ladder. Tilly and Gulnares' group followed. Ash reached the bottom of the ladder and stood looking down a dark corridor.

 Ash called out, "Hello... steward?" His voice echoing down the concrete tunnel, he shivered in the chill.

From down the long hallway a light flashed and a broken and crackling voice said, "Hello... is anyone... there?"

"We're right here\!" Called Tilly, "we're coming to help you."

The group ran down the dimly lit hallway; crackling sounds and a flashing light lit the steward. A bank of lights blinked on the console of the apparatus he had been repairing. He was melted into a braid of what looked like power cables. 

Ash approached the steward. "How do we help?" 

Tilly asked, "Are you hurt?"

"I am not... hurt, I do not feel pain," said the steward. "But I am trapped here."

Ash asked the steward, "Did something break? Is that why everything shut down?" 

Tilly tugged on Ash's leg and waved him down to listen to her. Ash bent down towards Tilly and she whispered in his ear, "He must have been trapped when we got here. We should ask if 'he' shut the power off," Tilly tilted her head, eyebrows raised, questioning.

Ash said, "steward, did you lock us down here?"

Gulnare growled, "is this a trap?" His group of Kithren looked at one another, a flash of action in their eyes.

The steward paused, "I did not mean to trap you in here with me..." his crackling voice faltering. "Something was drawing a lot of power... I had to shut it down... If the station runs out of power I will... die... I am trapped here." 

"Our friend Timothy needs a power-core," Ash told the steward. "He's stuck in the repair cabinet upstairs."

"Is your friend... a technician?" The steward asked.

"He is," said Tilly, "steward, what is this power station and what was the foundry, and what is a technician, what is all of this?"

---

The steward explained. "The sub-stations... help maintain the foundrys; the foundrys help the technicians... fulfill their protocols." The steward said in a broken crackling voice. "I should shut myself down... my function... is to help the technicians... the technicians repopulate the world... but... the longer I go without maintenance, the more bugs I get... the more I want to live..."  admitted the steward.

Ash said, "Steward, we will save you," with a serious look on his face.

Tilly interjected, "Ash,  we can't promise that. Steward, we can promise to uphold your choice."

Anora stepped forward raising her hands, "Quiet, I can hear the demon pigs." Snorts, grunts, and squeals were echoing through ventilation shafts. "Steward, something is driving the peccaries mad, even if we get out of here they will attack us. Do you know anything about this?"

The steward shook his head. "My ultrasonic sensor module is... failing. ...I am emitting high frequency sounds... this must be what is making them crazy." The steward paused, "so it is clear..."

Gulnare stepped towards the steward, "You must shut down for my people."

Anora got in front of him. "Dad, we can't ask him too." she said.

Ash looked at the steward and declared, "no, steward, we will find a way."

"No, steward," said Tilly, "we will honor your wishes\!" She looked at Gulnare and Ash defiantly.

The steward spoke up, "stop... everyone..." The power came back on, "It's clear that I have to... be shut down..."

Gulnare grabbed the stewards shoulder and said, "I am sorry robot, you are brave, I honor your spirit." Anora, and the rest of the Kithren nodded in agreement.

---

The lights came back on, Ash extinguished  the lantern and handed it to Tilly to put in her backpack. They could hear the noises of machines running coming from the sub-station above, then the echoes of Timothy climbing down the metal maintenance ladder. 

His footsteps got closer until he appeared in the doorway. Timothy looked at the steward and asked, "will we be able to save you? If you can't survive then this sub-station will go off-line possibly creating a  domino effect on other nodes."

"Timothy, not only will I not survive... but I need to be shut down immediately... to stop emitting the frequency that's driving the peccaries mad," said the steward. "I fear that there may be no way... to prevent a shutdown cascade event..." 

Tilly asked, "what happens if the Sub-Stations shut down? What do they do?" 

Timothy waited until he had everyone's attention, "there was a world before this one," he explained, "but all the life died... We have spent a long time re-seeding, bit by bit. The Foundry's and Sub-Stations were built by the people from before and now we use them to provide resources for the re-seeding. They built the stewards, the technicians and all the robots." 

Tilly, with real concern on her face, asked, "Did they all die?"

"Yes," said Timothy, "everyone and everything died." He waited, letting the statement linger. Then Timothy turned to the steward and said, "I have an idea, we could try to upload you to the Sub-Station. You would persist but without your physical body to perform maintenance the facility will fail. But this should give me time to reach the next facility." Timothy looked to Ash and Tilly and asked "you two are heading east? Should we all travel together?"

The peccaries could still be heard puffing and snorting through the vents. "We can't leave with them out there," said Gulnare.

"You're correct," said Timothy, "I will remove your chip steward and I'll install it in the foundry's main console." The steward leaned forward as Timothy approached.

Anora pushed towards the steward, "Wait\! Will this kill him?" A look of panic on her face.

"No, he will become part of the foundry," said Timothy. He removed the stewards chip, his robot form slumped forward. The noises from the peccaries echoing down the ventilation shafts went quiet. "Let me install the steward in the foundry then I will check outside to make sure it's safe." Timothy walked off down the hallway to the ladder.

Tilly and Anora paused in front of the stewards lifeless form. "Goodbye," said Tilly. Both Anora and Tilly gently put their hands on the robots cold metal frame. As Ash and the Kithren walked down the corridor they could hear Timothy climbing the metal ladder to the station above.

---

Ash climbed out of the maintenance shaft following Gulnare and his companions. Timothy stood at a console inputting information. Machines started to whir to life; consoles blinked and beeped. 

Timothy stepped back from the console, "Steward, are you there?" 

Tilly and Anora were the last to climb up the ladder, everyone paused, waiting for his response.

"I'm here," the stewards' voice came through the intercom. 

Everyone cheered, laughing and patting each other's backs.

Timothy looked at Gulnare and gave a quick nod then he strode to the door and walked outside. He walked back in and said, "they are gone\!" Everyone cheered again. 

"Thank you all," said Gulnare. "We can now return home. The three of you saved us and our village, If you want to come back to our village, we don't have much, but we can repay you with hospitality."

"Thank you Gulnare but I need to go east." Timothy told him.

"We're sticking with him," said Ash, "I'm so glad we met you and that we could help, though in truth, we all helped each other. It took all of us to solve the problem."

Gulnare and Anora and the rest walked off into the forest. Timothy, Tilly and Ash watched them go, Anora turned and waved, all three waved back. The early morning light gave Anora a magical glow.

Timothy turned and walked back into the sub-station, "steward, thank you for your service," He said.

The stewards voice crackled over the intercom again, "thank you Timothy, and Ash and... Tilly, I would have just... shut down alone if you hadn't found me. I'll be able to keep the sub-station running from here... for a little while, certainly long enough for you to get to the next node. I wish you all luck, thank you."

The three of them left the sub-station crunching across the leaf litter on the forest floor. Soft damp smells drifted as they walked. Chickadees landed on branches singing their chicka-dee-dee song. 

---

Miles ahead a lone figure stood by a low fire. His inky blue armor scratched and dented, he faced his captives. The bandits were tied to a tree, whimpering. 

"Please let me go," one of them begged. The air was thick with the smell of iron.

The Gloam Knight turned back to the fire and sat down; He didn't answer their pleas. He drew his sword and started cleaning it, the sword's blade reflecting in the firelight. They would find no mercy here, not tonight.
