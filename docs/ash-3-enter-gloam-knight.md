**Enter Gloam Knight**

Tilly was hunched over at a small stand of mushrooms foraging their next meal. She could smell the damp moss and browning leaf litter. The setting sun hurried her picking and collecting. Putting the last of the mushrooms into her sack, she walked back to the camp.

Timothy and Ash sat by a crackling fire. Tilly walked up and opened her pouch to show off her spoils, it was filled with a mix of berries, mushrooms and greens. 

"Looks good\!" Ash pulled jerky and dried bread from his pack.

Tilly got out bowls and started dividing the food between them, leaving some for breakfast. A thought occurred to her, she asked, “Timothy, how old are you?”

“I’m not sure,” he replied. “Thousands of years I’d guess. The more time that goes by the more my system is corrupted, every fix and rewrite is a chance for further corruption. I imagine that I knew at some point in the past.”

“Does that mean you’ll eventually forget who you are?” Ash questioned.

“I could,” Timothy replied. “Some forget who they are, some forget their purpose, some malfunction and just shut down.”

Tilly handed Ash a bowl, she was contemplating the implications of living so long, Ash swapped with her pieces of bread and jerky. 

Ash was thinking about the many ways that a robot could unravel, if any system could be corrupted at any time. They both ate, satiating their hunger from the day's trek.

They laid out their packrolls and tucked in for the night. Timothy too shut down his system for what he called his, “nightly defragment”. 

                                                              \*\*\*

The sun woke them shining through the trees just cresting the mountains in the distance. Tilly tossed some berries on the  ground near camp for the birds to eat. Small songbirds were flying and twittering about; juncos, chickadees, and nuthatches, all flew about singing their songs. Some picked at and ate the small berries, Tilly watched them in reverence. 

Tilly and Ash rolled and tied their pack rolls and all three of them sat by the extinguished fire while they ate the rest of their food from the night before. A brave chickadee landed on Timothy's shoulder, they all laughed in sweet surprise. Startled, the chickadee flew away.

“Well, I guess that’s our cue to leave.” Said Ash, standing he slung his pack over his shoulder. Tilly and Timothy stood too, Tilly suiting up her backpack and packroll. They all walked towards the road leaving the small birds to pick up any scraps from the encampment.

Walking out of the woods they stepped back onto the road. Ash stretched looking east, walking hadn’t been bad, they seemed to have found a good pace. Tilly was fast, even with her short strides She stayed between Ash, in the rear, and Timothy in the lead.

 A robin hopped around the base of a sign beside the road, it found a worm pulling it from the ground and flying away. Timothy stopped looking at the sign; it was a single stake with a board nailed to the top. On the board was a sun half behind mountains. It was a simple design, white paint on bare wood.

“Have either of you heard of the Gloam Knight?” Timothy asked them.

“No,” they both replied in unison.

“Well this is his symbol, he patrols this section of the road.” Timothy paused and looked at Tilly, then he looked at Ash. “He protects innocent people but he isn’t a good knight, he has bad intentions.” 

Ash and Tilly glanced at one another, Ash had never heard of a bad knight, well, Timothy did say that he protected people. But he also said that he had ‘bad intentions’ whatever that would mean.

They kept walking through the midday heat, sun directly above. A few miles ahead the road entered a forest that would offer them shade, he and Tilly had both taken off their cloaks and had rolled up their sleeves. Sweat soaked Ash’s shirt and hair. 

An eagle circled far above in the sky, Ash closed his eyes trying to imagine its view. For a moment he was there, high above the long stretch of road, fields and trees then mountains stretching away to either side. He imagined the cool air blowing against him as he flew in the sky. He looked upon the forest ahead but couldn’t see what lay beyond.

Ash looked at Tilly and Timothy thinking about how lucky he was to have met his companions. It felt like the fates themselves were pushing him forward; prompting every move as if on script of some magical story.

Ash heard the familiar call from a morning dove up ahead, ‘who, who, who-a-who, who’. Two doves were together pecking at the dirt at the road's entrance to the forest. Like a streak, something shot out of the sky and crashed into them. A cloud of road dust and feathers erupted from the ground. An eagle flew off in one direction, a lone morning flew in another.

Ash ran over to investigate but all that was left was some scuffled dirt and feathers, a few of which were still drifting to the ground when he got to the spot where the doves had been. “Well, I guess an eagle’s gotta eat too,” he said, shrugging. Tilly shook her head yes, giving Ash a solemn look.

They entered the forest greeted by the shade and a cooling breeze. A clanging noise echoed through the forest; jarring metal striking something, clang, clang, clang\!

Ash noticed a torn piece of cloth hanging from a branch, it was spattered with dark stains. He cringed a bit hoping the stains weren’t blood..

“What is that noise\!” Tilly was clearly bothered by the banging, her furry ears twitching with every knock. “It’s just creepy, it is knocking but it’s inviting us to go towards it and it feels off, like it’s leading towards something bad.”

“It sounds like it could be metal striking metal, against a rock?” Timothy said, alert and listening. “It does feel ominous, it's very unnatural to hear in the forest.”

Ash wondered if it had something to do with the Gloam Knight. It sounded bad, the clang, clang. He peered through the trees, eyes vigilant. Searching for, he didn’t know what. But they were all on edge, walking quietly, they stopped talking.

Then the clanging ended. “I think it stopped,” said Tilly, relaxing her shoulders. But she noted that no birds were singing, no chipmunks chattering. They continued on, the silence feeling just as unnatural as the clanging metal sound did.

                                                       \*\*\*

With only the sound of their footfalls crunching in the dirt they walked in Eerie silence.

Timothy halted, putting his hands out then raising a finger to his mouth; they all stopped and listened. Tilly could hear a high pitched ringing amidst the lack of noise. They all scanned through the trees. Timothy was staring off in a specific direction, Ash and Tilly noticed and started looking in that direction too.

“I thought I heard something,” said Timothy, still staring off into the trees.

Ash noticed Tilly’s ears twitching and Timothy said, “that’s it, someone's coming.” Then Ash could hear it too, footsteps crunching through the woods, getting louder as they approached. 

A man came into view. Tilly was startled when she saw him. He was walking right towards them.

“Hello.” Ash called, clearly nervous. As the character came closer Ash could see he was a knight. His cloak and armor, deep blue. 

In a quiet voice Timothy said, “This is Gloam Knight, he is scary but we’re not in danger. He doesn’t know who I am.”

“Hello travelers,” The knight said loudly, almost antagonistically, as he approached. “Are you all just traveling through?” His gruff voice landed like a terrible threat. The knight was walking fast, nearly upon them. He was fully armored, helmet to boots he was covered. All a shiny dark blue purple-ish hue, like an empty night sky after sunset, as if there should be stars.  

“We are traveling through,” said Timothy, “I am escorting my young friends here to the east. They want to apprentice under the knights Artorius and Siff.” 

“Okay, well, you all don’t look like trouble.” The gloam knight walked up, towering over them, he looked them all up and down. “I’ll escort you, this road can be dangerous, ‘lots of bandits about.”   
“Great,” Timothy said, “better safe than sorry.” He looked at Ash and Tilly, they both smiled nervously.

They continued on, the sound of the Gloam knights jangling armor now a part of their sounds as they moved through the creepily quiet forest.
