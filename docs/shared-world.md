# The Shared World

*Green Horn and the Ash stories. Written 2026-09-04.*

Green Horn is a game. The Ash stories are prose. They are **the same world, and
the same plan, read at two points on the clock** — and that relationship is
worth writing down, because it decides what the game is allowed to mean.

This doc exists to answer one question when it comes up: *is that canon?*

---

## The clock

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** | Humans build a photosynthesis technology to repair the atmosphere | *implied* |
| **The overshoot** | It works, and keeps working. Oxygen climbs past 35%. Humans cannot live at 35% — pulmonary oxygen toxicity. Everyone dies | *implied* |
| **Green Horn** | Arthropods have grown enormous in the oxygen. Robots execute the plan: bring the number down, bring humanity back. **You are a unit sent out into that gap.** | the game |
| **…thousands of years…** | Nodes fail one by one. Technicians are reactivated, repaired, copied from copies | |
| **Ash** | The re-seeding has worked. Chickadees, junco, nuthatch, deer, beaver, mink, bear. Villages. Kithren. The remaining technicians still walk their routes | the stories |

Timothy, in [ash-3-enter-gloam-knight.md](ash-3-enter-gloam-knight.md), puts his
own age at *"thousands of years I'd guess."*

## Timothy is the protagonist, at the end of his run

This is the most valuable thing the stories give the game, and it is worth
being explicit about.

> **green-horn-story-fragments.md:** *"A unit sent out into the gap. It will
> rust away long before the job is done, get backed up, and be sent out
> again."*

> **Ash 3:** *"The more time that goes by the more my system is corrupted,
> every fix and rewrite is a chance for further corruption... Some forget who
> they are, some forget their purpose, some malfunction and just shut down."*

That is not a similar character. It is the **same role, thousands of
reactivations later** — a technician, walking node to node, power core
failing, held together by fabricators, running a copy of a copy of a copy.

The Miscalculation section of the story fragments lists *"the math has drift"*
as one of three candidate themes. Ash 3 does not propose it. It has already
happened, and it has a face.

### What that buys

**The game does not have to resolve anything.** The story doc's governing
principle is *"a game that concludes killing is wrong stops being interesting
the moment you understand it."* The resolution already exists, thousands of
years downstream, and it is neither triumphant nor damning:

The re-seeding worked. There are chickadees. And the machine that did it can
no longer quite remember why.

That lets the game stay unresolved without feeling evasive, because the world
it belongs to has somewhere gentle to land.

**It also sets the emotional ceiling.** Green Horn can be grim. It is a run
through an ecosystem you are clearing, with a rust clock and no company. It
can afford that, because this is where it ends up.

## The recurring shape: a misread threat

Four times, across both projects, in the same shape.

| | Looks like | Actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **Grimelda** ([ash-1](ash-1-apprentice-to-the-east.md)) | A witch stealing livestock and taking a girl | A mink took the cuy. A beaver flooded the swamp. Imogen fell in the dam chasing the mink. Grimelda tells them where she is |
| **The demon pigs** ([ash-2](ash-2-echoes-in-the-sub-station.md)) | Fifty peccaries besieging a station | A failing ultrasonic sensor on a robot nobody suspects |
| **The pacifist bugs** (game) | Identical to the thing killing you | Never attacks, ever |
| **The oxygen** (premise) | Arthropods overrunning the world | A machine overshot and they grew into the room it made |

The second one is the fourth one **at village scale**: a broken machine makes
creatures behave in a way that reads as malice, everybody blames the
creatures, and the fix is to shut the machine down — not to kill the pigs.

*Echoes in the Sub-Station* is therefore the game's premise told as a short
story, and it already ends correctly. That is not a coincidence to be pleased
about; it is a spine. **This shape is what the work is about.** Anything that
contradicts it is probably wrong, and anything that repeats it is probably
right.

It also answers a question the game had open: **what is in the middle of the
arena?** Not a boss. The thing that made the beetles.

## Persistence, already dramatised

The story fragments commit to *death is diegetic — robots have backups*.
Ash 2 shows what that costs:

> *"we could try to upload you to the Sub-Station. You would persist but
> without your physical body to perform maintenance the facility will fail."*

**You persist, and the place that holds you degrades for it.** That is a
better meta-progression system than a retained item, and it is already the
fiction. Anora asks the player's question first — *"Wait! Will this kill
him?"*

Keep the steward's line, wherever it ends up:

> *"the longer I go without maintenance, the more bugs I get... the more I
> want to live."*

Degradation producing a will to live. That is the Miscalculation theme in one
sentence, spoken by a machine that is about to choose to stop.

## The stations are already the systems

Nothing here needs inventing. It needs taking.

| Fiction | System |
|---|---|
| Foundry-node 47, sub-station-node 50, shutdown cascade | The run structure — a route between nodes, each one failing |
| The maintenance chamber Timothy steps into | The fabricator. Bring carapace, get plating. No currency in a world that has none |
| A technician crossing open country before his core dies | The run, with rust as the reason not to dawdle |
| The steward's chip in the console | Meta-progression, with a price |
| Cleaning bots waking as a room powers on | What "activating a node" looks like |

## Canon: settled, and open

**Settled.**

- Same world, same plan, one clock.
- Humans are gone and are not coming back during Green Horn.
- The technicians are the same role in both. Timothy is what the player
  becomes if the player never stops.
- Robots degrade by copying. Some forget who they are. Some choose to stop.
- Threats are misread. The cause is usually a machine, and usually nobody's
  fault.

**Open, and worth deciding before they get decided by accident.**

1. **Do the Kithren exist during Green Horn?** In Ash they are a settled
   people with villages and traditions. If they exist during the game they
   are *early* — and one of them, met once, seen and not explained, would be
   the first evidence the player has that any of this is working. The story
   fragments already allow that *"humans are a step rather than a
   destination."* The Kithren are that with a face on it.
2. **Does the player ever learn the oxygen number is stale?** Or is that
   knowledge reserved for the reader of the stories?
3. **Is Grimelda a person, a robot, or something the re-seeding produced?**
   She sedates a man by raising a hand and disappears into mist. Nothing in
   either project explains it yet, and it may be better unexplained.
4. **Does the game ever show a working village?** Ash's Longbrook is the
   emotional opposite of a Green Horn run. Showing it would resolve the
   question the game is trying not to resolve.
5. **How much does the player know about the plan?** Open question 1 of the
   story fragments, unchanged, and it decides the tone of every line of text
   in the game.
